# Ashes of Eden — notes for AI assistants

Godot 4.7 / GDScript, 2D pixel action roguelite. Open source, community-driven, 4 locales (en, ru, uk, zh_CN).

## Conventions
- Content is data: `data/**/*.json` (abilities, enemies, dialogues), strings in `localization/strings.csv`. Never hardcode player-facing text; use a key and add all 4 columns.
- Autoloads: `EventBus` (signals), `Data` (JSON loader), `Game` (run state: hidden alignment grace/temptation/will, flags, gifts), `Settings` (locale).
- New ability effect type → `scripts/combat/ability_system.gd` **and** `scripts/tools/validate_data.gd`. A new gift *mechanic* is usually a new key in `Player.BASE_STATS` (default 0 = no change) plus the code that reads it: also add it to `STATS` in the validator, a cap in `AbilitySystem.CAPS`, and a check in `gift_test.gd`. Every gift has a `rarity`. Same for enemy behaviours / attack types (`scripts/enemies/enemy.gd`).
- Rooms are written by `tools/rooms/generate_rooms.py` from its tables and `tools/rooms/painted_rooms.py`, and the tables are the rooms: fix a room there and regenerate, never in the editor alone — `tools/check_generators.py` (CI) fails when a scene is not what its table writes. A painted room's table is in panel pixels; a thing standing inside an inserted seam, which no panel x maps to, takes a fourth field `"wide"` (widened-room x). A `TRAVERSAL_PATCHES` entry may carry a fifth field, its node name (`<name>Shape`, `<name>Cornice`), for a test to find it by.
- **Rooms made of several paintings** (docs/ROOM_COMPOSER.md, `tools/rooms/compose.py`, the studio's «Конструктор»): `tools/rooms/composed_rooms.json` lays 1280×720 paintings on a grid (≤ 6×4; the game's own, or `assets/levels/panels/<stem>.png`), every coordinate in finished-room pixels, keyed by the painted room whose atmosphere it keeps; the generator stitches the seams (`_join`) into `assets/levels/composed/<name>.png`. A draft is `scenes/rooms/composed/<name>.tscn`, opened with `room=<name> --composed` (no autosave); `"live": true` makes it the room and check_reach must hold. Its backdrop rule is `composed/<name>` (the room's flames, no zones until it has its own). Unless the entry has its own `windows` / `lights` / `front`, it takes its cells' rooms' far openings (onto a plate stitched from their `depth/<room>_backfill.png`) and candles; a hole in the grid is its neighbour mirrored into night. The studio's reach check, ledge tracer and stitch are ports (`composeReach`, `traceLedges`, `composeTiles` in lib.js; lib.test.js runs compose.py against the stitch): change painted_rooms.check_reach, trace_panel.ledges or compose.py, change them. The engine tests take `-- --composed [room]` (reach, stairs, void_fall), which the studio's «✓ Проверить в Godot» runs. `test_compose.py`.
- Bestiary strips are cut from the generated atlases by `tools/art/build_bestiary_assets.py` as whole shapes, not a hard grid: a swing that crosses the atlas grid stays in its frame, a neighbour's blade stays out, and the cell grows to fit (written into `tree.json`). Regenerate, don't hand-edit; the validator checks every strip against its cell and that every ranged enemy attack has an animated `projectile_style`.
- **Data comes from roots, not from `res://data`** (`scripts/data/data_doc.gd`, docs/GAME_PACKS.md): the engine's `res://data`, then a game pack checked out into `res://content/data`, then mods. Collections overlay by id (`Data._load_collection`); a one-document file (`hero.json`, `game.json`, `projectiles.json`, `action_fx.json`, `gore.json`, `backdrops.json`, `ambience.json`, `dialogue_looks.json`, `player_actions.json`, `foliage.json`, `music/playlists.json`) is read with `DataDoc.doc(...)`, deep-merged — never `FileAccess` on `res://data/…` in game code. A pack's `game.json` `inherit` names the collections it takes from the engine; the rest are its own. The validator checks the merged game. `data_roots_test.gd`.
- **What makes Ashes Ashes is one file** (`data/game.json`, docs/GAME_PACKS.md): the rooms in play order, the practice yard, the menu's picture and music, and the three paths' `color` (cards, text), `aura` (the light and marks on the hero), `drift` and `icon` — read through `Data.game`, `Data.rooms`, `Data.path_color` / `path_drift` / `path_icon`. Code never lists rooms or paints a path itself; `_check_game` in the validator checks every file it names. The path ids stay grace / temptation / will (the engine counts them); their names are `PATH_<ID>` strings.
- **Other games are packs** (docs/GAME_PACKS.md, `tools/packs/`): a game's own repository (`make new-game`) laid over the engine as `content/` (`make game GAME=…`, `apply_pack.py`, which also merges its strings and names the project; `make ashes` undoes it). Its `game.json` says what it takes (`inherit`, `exclude`), how it opens (`opening`: prologue / intro / last_fall ids — never name a scene id of Ashes in code) and which flags only code reads (`flags_read`). Raise `DataDoc.ENGINE` when a format change would break an older pack. CI for a pack is the engine's reusable `.github/workflows/game.yml` (validate + `game_smoke_test.gd`, Web build, the studio's data on its `studio-data` branch); the studio opens one with `?game=owner/name` or `serve.py --game DIR`, and a send goes through `packFiles`. A pack's rooms are its tables (`rooms/rooms.json`: an engine room as `base` with the pack's spawns and weather; `rooms/composed_rooms.json`; `rooms/studio_rooms.json`), written by `generate_rooms.py --pack` into `content/scenes/rooms/` with `base_room`, whose look `RoomLayers.look_key` takes from the base; its Chinese is its own subset cut from the merged strings (`make_cjk_font.py --pack`), which `apply_pack.py` copies over the engine's — never lay a font into the theme's fallbacks at run time (Godot segfaults on exit under Linux); the studio's drafts are per game (`storageKey`). `test_packs.py`.
- **How the hero handles is data** (`data/hero.json`, docs/GAME_PACKS.md): `stats` is `Player.BASE_STATS`, `feel` the timings and reaches (`PARRY_WINDOW`, `COMBO_MULTIPLIERS`, `SLAM_SPEED`, … — `Player.FEEL_KEYS`), `static var`s that `Player.apply_hero` lays on when `Data` loads (`Data.load_doc`, every root merged). The numbers in player.gd are only the fallback for a key a file leaves out: tune the file, not the script. A new feel number → a `static var`, its name in `FEEL_KEYS`, its key in hero.json (`hero_data_test.gd` wants the same keys in both; `_check_hero` in the validator bounds them). Two more sections make it the whole fight, per game: `moves` (`Player.MOVE_KEYS`, read through `Player.can_move` — a move turned off leaves its button doing the plain thing, and the yard's list and the hints skip it) and `enemy_ai` (`Enemy.AI_KEYS`: the spacing and waking numbers of docs/ENEMY_AI.md, `static var`s laid on by `Enemy.apply_ai`; one enemy may override with `"ai"` in its JSON, read by `Enemy._ai` — use it, not the bare static, in per-body logic). `combat_data_test.gd`. The studio's «Боёвка» edits both files and feeds the sandbox (`hero` in the live post).
- Hero frames: `assets/sprites/elian_frames.tres` + `slash_frames.tres` (128×64 cells, feet at x=68 on the bottom row, body 43 px tall — a hair under the village knight; effects 80×48). Regenerate with `tools/art/slice_hero.py --contact` (one entry per generated sheet in `SHEETS`; check `tools/art/contact/hero.png`) then `tools/art/build_elian_frames.py`; don't hand-edit. The player's `Body` sits at (0,−20): the cell's bottom row is 4 px above the collision's bottom, the same lift the enemies have, so his boots clear the foreground grass like theirs.
- Hero animations the code relies on (`scripts/player/player.gd`): `rest`/`draw`/`idle` (sword sheathed until an enemy is aware or we swing), `attack`/`attack2`/`attack3` (the chain), `thrust` (backstab pose), `rising` (airborne swing), `dash_strike` (swing out of a roll), `jump`/`fall`/`climb`/`land`, `hurt`/`knockback`, `roll`, `death`, `wake`, `talk`, `guard`. The swings have their white arc drawn in; the `Slash` sprite only adds the path colour (`gold`/`red`) and the finisher's `heavy` arc.
- An enemy's strips are named as `Enemy` plays them (`ANIMATIONS` in the validator, `ENEMY_ANIMATIONS` in the studio's lib.js — a test holds them equal). `idle_to_walk` is optional: drawn, it plays once when the body rises from a rest it held (`IDLE_TO_WALK_REST`) into the walk, then the walk (`Enemy._play`, `_on_sprite_finished`); a blow cuts it short. The studio gives a bestiary character an animation it lacks as a strip of its own plus a line in its `sprite.animations` in the tree (`frameEdits` `add`, `resizedStrips`). `studio_live_test.gd`.
- Every enemy attack has a wind-up the player can read; never add an unreadable instant hit. A variant on an old strip is `"extends"` + `sprite.like` (and a tint), not new art; `shield` (≤ 0.8, turned from the front, open from the wind-up to the end of the recovery, never against a backstab or riposte) and `on_death: {type: split, into, count}` are the data mechanics for one. `variants_test.gd`.
- **Particles on actions are data** (`data/action_fx.json`, `scripts/fx/action_fx.gd`): an `ActionFx` node on the hero's and every creature's sprite fires emitters (Fx dust / puff / sparkle / ash / debris / ring / flash, from feet / body / head / hand / back) at an animation's start, on chosen frames, or every so often, and for the events code sends with `ActionFx.event(self, …)`: step, jump, land, roll, wake. The hero's footfall, landing, roll and waking dust live there now — add a new cosmetic burst on an action here, not as an `Fx` call in player.gd. The studio's «Частицы» edits it. `action_fx_test.gd`.
- **A bolt's look is data** (`data/projectiles.json`, read by `Projectile.style`): strip, frames, fps, scale, light, trail, flight shader, and the animation keys pulse / breathe / squash / bob / wiggle / spin, an `echo` and a `burst`. A ranged attack (and a `bolt` gift: `style`, `motion`, …) names a style and may add `volley` + `volley_gap` (rounds, each aimed anew), `speed_jitter`, `projectile_scale`, and the motion `home` (turns toward its quarry at `motion_amount` rad/s, ≤ 4); the validator bounds them all. `blade` (the swing wave) and `sacred` (a parried bolt) are the game's own and must stay. The studio's «Снаряды» edits all of it and previews it with a port of `projectile.gd`'s flight — change one, change the other. `volley_test.gd`, `projectile_fx_test.gd`, `projectile_motion_test.gd`.
- Enemies fight at a distance (docs/ENEMY_AI.md): a walker paces a band just outside its reach and steps in only when a blow is ready and it holds one of two slots on its side (the rest queue further back), then gives ground; a flyer hovers on a ring, closes in only to strike, and is pushed off the hero's body otherwise. `enemy_spacing_test.gd` measures it; don't bring back the stand-in-his-face chase.
- An elite (`"elite"` in `tags`, not a boss; all of it in docs/ELITES.md) reads as one before it swings: `Enemy._mark_elite` puts a low ember `GlowLight` under it (`EliteMark`) and lets a mote rise off it now and then (`EliteMotes`); a tint alone was too quiet. `vials_test.gd` checks the mark. Its fall leaves a cache (`Enemy._leave_cache`, prop `elite_cache`: a common item and some essence) on the floor under the body, laid on every peer as it sees the death, never in the yard; a flyer over a pit leaves none. `elite_cache_test.gd`. An elite also rises with an affix (`data/affixes`: Ironclad, Swift, Brutal, Enduring — `mods` multiply hp, damage, speed and the cooldown between blows, add armour; never a wind-up): the host rolls it in `Run.roll_affix` (none for commons, bosses or the yard) and carries it in the spawn data, so every peer builds the same body (`Enemy._apply_affix`); its name stands over the head, lifted back from the room's `Ambient` night; laid low, it is kept on the elite's bestiary page (`Profile.record_affix`, "Beaten as"). `affix_test.gd`.
- **Blood and torn limbs are data** (`data/gore.json`, `scripts/fx/gore.gd`, the studio's «Кровь»): a table per `material`, a body's own keys (`bodies.<elian | enemy id>`) laid over it. `Gore.hit` (from `Enemy._hit_fx` / `Player._hit_fx`) throws drops along the blow and leaves a dripping wound; `Gore.death` (from `_die` / `_go_down`) throws a gush, grows a pool, and with `limbs` ≥ 2 and its `chance` (×2 for a crit or backstab) cuts the frame the body died in into pieces (`Gore.cut`, `GorePiece`) — the caller then hides the body. No two alike: `cuts` weighs the ways (`all` to pieces, `head` off, `half` at the waist, `limb` an arm off — a body of ≤ 4 pieces loses its head instead; `Gore.pick_cut`), cut lines wander by `RAGGED`, what is left standing (`stands`) holds a beat and topples the way the blow went, a stump jets in weakening pulses for `spurt` seconds, every drop and mark takes its own `shade`, some drops are two-pixel clots, marks are smears, splats or broken trails, a pool is several spreads, and counts, tilt and speed are rolled per blow. Drops and pieces are stopped by the room's world and ledge layers by ray, marks lie on one `Gore` layer per room; blood keeps to what it lands on (`Gore.surface`: the box a ray hit) — a mark stops at its ledge's edge and drips off it (`drip`), a streak runs down a wall by `run` and never past its foot, a ledge's underside drips, a heavy drop splashes (`splash`), a share flies into the depth and sticks to the painting (`back`) but never inside an opening (`RoomLayers.window_outline`), `mist` leaves no mark, and `soak` multiplies a mark into the picture under it (the `Soak` child, `BLEND_MODE_MUL`); a back splat also needs something solid near it (`_near_solid`, `BACK_REACH`: a wall, a pillar, a ledge overhead), else it falls like any drop — a splat on a far hill is a blot on the picture; floor marks are ragged rows across the floor's painted top face (lifted 0–2 px, the collider's top is its far edge), and a drop that falls into a `Hazard` pool stops at its surface, stirs it (`surface.disturb`) and clouds in water (`cloud`) or hisses off as steam on what burns (`hiss`, `Hazard.spec_of(kind).burns`); bosses and Elian are never torn. **The blow shapes it**: `moves.<move>` (`Gore.MOVES`: the chain, rising, thrust, dash_strike, lunge, cleave, sweep, slam, dash, the skills) turns the spray (`aim`, by the side the blow went), fans or narrows it, sets the wound's `height`, `force`, `drops`, `jet`, `pool`, the tear's `chance` and `cuts`, and how the pieces fly (`fling`, `lift`, `scatter`) — the uppercut throws the head up, the slam scatters all ways, the thrust is one jet; the move rides in the hit's `info.move` (`Gore.move_spec`, `MOVE_DEFAULTS` for the rest; the validator reads the names from gore.gd's text). Cosmetic and local on every peer. `Settings.gore` (full / reduced: half the drops, no mist, no splashes, nobody torn / off), Settings → Blood. The studio's preview ports gore.gd (its «Приём» picks the blow, water and lava lie in its floor): change one, change the other. `gore_test.gd`; `gore_shot.gd` (needs a display) kills a body by each move in real rooms and saves strips to look at.
- A death leaves what the body was made of (`Fx.remains` by `material`: blood, rags, shards, wisps, feathers, stars): 1:1 pixel shapes (`Fx.PIXELS`), never a soft dot scaled up into a square. A blast shows its reach with `Fx.ring`, not a magnified smoke sprite.
- Enemies start unaware (`PATROL`): they pace around their spawn and only hunt once they see a player (cone in front + raycast, `sight`/`patrol` in the JSON), get touched, hit, or a neighbour shouts. A hit on an unaware enemy is a backstab (`Player.stats.backstab_multiplier`, decided by the enemy/host in `_apply_damage`). Bosses and boss summons start aware.
- Settings live in the `Settings` autoload (locale, bus volumes, fullscreen, shake, key overrides). Accessibility: `text_size` (normal / large / largest, folded into `Settings.text_scale()` with the touch factor — size new story text, hints and toasts by it) and `flashes` (`Settings.flash_scale()`: full 1, reduced 0.35 — `Fx.flash`, the white of a blow, the hurt vignette; a new flash reads it too). `accessibility_test.gd`. `auto_attack` (off by default): `Player._auto_swing` swings at the nearest *awake* enemy within `AUTO_REACH`, turning to it — never at a sleeper, so the backstab stays a choice; `auto_attack_test.gd`. `game_speed` (`GAME_SPEEDS` 100–60 %): `Run` hands `Settings.time_scale()` to `Juice.set_base_scale`, which holds `Engine.time_scale` there and gives it back after a hit-stop; full speed online, in the night of the day and in a tool script unless it asks (`time_scale(true)`) — never set `Engine.time_scale` anywhere else. `block_toggle` (off by default): one press of `block` raises the guard and the next lowers it (`Player._block_toggled`, cleared on revive); the raising press is still fresh, so the parry window is the same — `parry_test.gd`. `damage_numbers` (on by default): `Fx.damage_number` draws nothing when off — the blow still flashes, staggers and sounds. `Settings.vibration`: `Juice.buzz` shakes the phone and every connected gamepad (light motor for small blows, heavy as they grow) on our own blow taken, a parry, our death, a boss down; `last_buzz` is what the test reads. New audio goes to the `Music` or `SFX` bus, never Master.
- Every enemy sounds different when you hit it: its `material` in the JSON picks the impact clips (`<id>_impact_1..5`, fallback `hit_<material>`, generic `hit` for props). Whoever swung plays it (`Enemy.impact_sound()` asked from `Player._attack`), so it lands with no round trip online; the creature's own cry (`<id>_hurt`) is separate. Every cue comes in three takes (two for the death rattle) — one throat, re-rolled — so a crowd of one kind is still a crowd. Regenerate with `tools/audio/generate_voices.py`; it is deterministic, so a regeneration is not a diff.
- Music is a mood, not a file: `Audio.music("graveyard")` picks from `data/music/playlists.json` (157 tracks, 11 moods, never the same twice in a row). New track → a row in `tools/audio/music_manifest.json` (free/attribution-compatible source, its playlists) then `python3 tools/audio/fetch_music.py`, which downloads, trims to 150 s, normalises to −18 LUFS and rewrites the playlists and `assets/CREDITS.md`. Verify a candidate URL before adding it: a 404 is the usual failure. After changing the library run `python3 tools/export/music_filters.py`: Android and Web presets carry only a light subset, and `Audio._pick` skips tracks a build does not have.
- Sound is a name, not a file: `Audio.play(&"hit")`, `Audio.play_at(&"enemy_death", pos)`, `Audio.music("graveyard")`. A row in `MIX` (level, pitch, spread) in `scripts/autoload/audio.gd` balances it, a row in `assets/CREDITS.md` credits it. Third-party audio is CC0 only; loudness and pitch are set in code, never baked into the file.
- **Takes, not clips.** A name ending in `_<number>` is a take of the name before it (`Audio._base_name`), and asking for the bare name plays one of them at random — that is the whole cure for a fight that sounds like a loop. `swing`, `step`, `block`, `parry`, `enemy_swing`, `draw` and every creature cue have several. Extra takes from CC0 packs → a row in `tools/audio/sfx_manifest.json` then `python3 tools/audio/fetch_sfx.py` (downloads the pack once, peak-normalises the members, writes `<name>_10…` and the credits). Placeholders with no real clip yet come from `tools/audio/generate_sfx.py`.
- **Story lines are spoken, and nothing depends on it.** `Audio.speak("DLG_…")` looks for `assets/audio/voice/<locale>/<KEY>.mp3` and returns its length, or 0.0 when nobody recorded it; `dialogue_box.gd` holds the caption for whichever is longer. A missing file is not a bug — an unvoiced line is a caption, which is how the game played before any of this. Who sounds like whom is `tools/audio/voice_cast.json`, rendered by `python3 tools/audio/generate_speech.py` (Piper, local, offline). **A rendered line inherits the licence of the voice's dataset**, and `LICENSE-ASSETS.md` bans non-commercial, so several of Piper's best voices (`en_US-ryan`, `ru_RU-ruslan`, `zh_CN-xiao_ya`, `en_US-lessac`) are refused by name in the script. Check a new voice's `MODEL_CARD` on `rhasspy/piper-voices` — the `.onnx.json` says nothing about licensing. Turned off by `Settings.speech` (Settings → Spoken lines), which `Audio.speak` checks first. Pitch shifting goes through the WORLD vocoder so formants stay put — a resampler drags them along and a voice four semitones down stops sounding like a person; `"formants": false` in the cast opts out, and only the two parts that are not people use it. Full story: `docs/VOICE.md`.
- **Ground slam and wall grab are free moves, not gifts.** Down in the air (`_start_slam`) drops him at `SLAM_SPEED` and hits everything beside where he lands for `SLAM_DAMAGE_SHARE` of a swing; it needs `SLAM_MIN_HEIGHT` of clear world below (`_room_to_slam`, world layer only — a jump-through ledge is not a floor to slam onto), and on the ground `down` is still the drop-through. Holding the stick into a wall in mid-air (`_update_wall_grab`) caps the fall at `WALL_SLIDE_SPEED` and lets `jump` push off away from it, handing back the air jump rather than adding one. Both are locked out during a roll, a mantle, a heal and a scripted pose, and both are cleared on death and revive. The touch stick already presses `move_down`, so neither needed a new button. `traversal_moves_test.gd` covers them.
- **A gift can arrive on a later night.** `"unlock_nights": N` in `data/abilities` keeps it out of `Run._roll_gifts` until `Profile.data.nights >= N` (`Run.gift_locked`), and on the night it arrives `Run._announce_unlocks` says so once over the opening. Ignored in a duel — both players must see the same cards. A gift without the field is unaffected, which is all of them but six. A chapter's gift or omen names its night instead (`"nights": ["euphrates"]`, `Omens.of_night`, read by `_roll_gifts` and `Omens.roll`; the validator checks the ids): it is dealt only there. A night's own gift weighs `Run.NIGHT_GIFT_WEIGHT` × its rarity — chapter V's sting-and-fruit gifts would be dead cards anywhere else.
- **The run's lean is audible, not only visible.** `Player._update_aura` already knows the dominant path and how far ahead it is; it hands both to `Audio.alignment_layer(path, strength)`, which crossfades a per-path loop (`assets/audio/music/layer_<path>.wav`, made by `tools/audio/generate_alignment_layers.py`) under whatever the room is playing. Silent below a lead of 2, and it fades out slower than it fades in so a brief swing does not make it flicker. The layers are not in any playlist, so `tools/export/music_filters.py` keeps them by name — otherwise light Android/Web builds would drop them. `alignment_test.gd` covers it (and does not trust `playing`: headless uses the dummy audio driver).
- **The world notices the habit.** `Profile.data.habit` counts nights in a row that leaned one way (`Game.lead() >= 2`, the aura's threshold); from `Profile.HABIT_NIGHTS` (3) on, `Profile.habit()` names the path. A dialogue router branch `{"habit": path}` (`DialogueBox.branch_holds`, never online) gives Elian's waking and the angel's greeting a line for it, and `_update_aura` marks the body at `HABIT_MARK` while the night itself is level. `habit_test.gd`.
- **The lean also shows on the body.** `Player._mark_body(path, strength)` binds `assets/shaders/alignment_marks.gdshader` to the sprite: edge light for grace, veins for temptation, ash and desaturation for will. It reads the sprite's own alpha (four taps for the silhouette edge) rather than a painted mask, so it holds through every animation and costs no art. Below a lead of 2 the material stays bound with `strength` 0 — a no-op, cheaper than swapping materials each frame. Covered by `alignment_test.gd` alongside the music layer.
- **The Chinese has its own face in the build.** Neither Latin font has a CJK glyph; a desktop hides that behind a system fallback, a Web export cannot. `assets/fonts/NotoSerifSC-Subset.ttf` — Noto Serif SC, instanced to weight 400 and cut down to the characters `strings.csv` actually uses by `tools/art/make_cjk_font.py` (25 MB → 343 KB) — sits in the theme's fallback chain ahead of the system font. Serif, not sans: the game is set in EB Garamond and Forum, and Song is what belongs beside them. Add Chinese text with a character outside the subset and `validate_data.gd` fails; rerun the tool and commit the result. Any other character the game can draw (every locale, and the non-ASCII literals in scripts, scenes and data) must be in EB Garamond or that subset too — `_check_glyphs` fails on one that is in neither, because the Web draws it as a box (`▸`, `✓`, `★` did); add a mark to `make_cjk_font.py`'s list or pick one EB Garamond has (`›`).
- **Story flags are checked in both directions.** `validate_data.gd` collects every flag written (`set_flags` in a dialogue choice or a prop) and every flag read (a router's `branches`, a cutscene step's `if`/`unless`, plus the game's `flags_read` in `data/game.json` for the ones read elsewhere). A flag read but never written is an error — that branch cannot be reached. A flag written but never read is a warning — a choice the world forgot to react to. Both fail silently in play, which is why they are caught here.
- Unique names (`%Name`) only resolve inside their own scene: from `run.gd` use `run_end.get_node("%RunEndStats")`, not `%RunEndStats`.
- Physics layers: 1 world (solid), 2 player, 3 enemies, 4 player hitbox, 5 ledges (one-way; walkers and the player collide with it, flyers don't, down+jump drops through). Side-view platformer (gravity in player/enemy scripts, not ProjectSettings).
- Level flow: `scenes/run/run.tscn` chains `scenes/rooms/*.tscn` in the order of `data/game.json` `rooms` (`Data.rooms`, which `Run.ROOMS` returns). Rooms are hand-made; enemies are `EnemySpawn` markers. Rooms have `width` and `height` (most are two screens tall, boss arenas one); the run sets the camera limits from them.
- A painted room has three planes (`scripts/rooms/room_layers.gd`): **far** — the inpainted plates behind the painting's openings (`DepthWindows`) and the clouds, clipped to the sky opening (`SkyClip`); only these slide with the camera, and only the openings listed in `RoomLayers.FAR_WINDOWS` (a recess in masonry or water at the hero's level stays still); **middle** — the painting and all that stands on it, with fog anchored to the world; **front** — `DecorFront`, weather, ambience, and the **front plane** (docs/DEPTH.md): pieces cut from the room's own pictures by `tools/rooms/front.py` (keyed dark or by alpha, darkened, scaled by their depth, softened, edges feathered) into `assets/levels/front/`, standing under a `FrontLayer` (z 3, no light) that slides each one faster than the camera (`rest + (rest − camera)·(depth − 1)`) and fades it while a body is behind it; a painted room's pieces are `tools/rooms/front_layers.json`, a composed room's its entry's `front`. ## Multiplayer (see docs/MULTIPLAYER.md)
- Two players, online, crossplay. Transport is WebSocket (`Net` autoload) because it is the only Godot peer that works in a Web export; do not reach for ENet.
- The host simulates rooms, enemies and score; every player body is simulated by its owner. A body is only ever hurt by its owner — `take_damage` forwards the hit instead of applying it when it is not.
- Never `get_tree().paused` — call `Net.set_paused`. Pausing freezes the synchronizers and desyncs the session.
- There may be two players in the tree: no `get_first_node_in_group("player")`; pick by `is_multiplayer_authority()` or act on all of them.
- Replicated properties are declared in code (`Net.attach_sync`, called from the spawner's spawn function before the node enters the tree), not in a `.tscn`.
- New enemy theatre (a telegraph, a swing, a beam) → an RPC next to `_net_strike`, guarded by `if Net.active`.
- **Rooms by code, from anywhere** (docs/RELAY.md): what ZeroTier/Radmin are used for, inside the game. Both players dial out to the relay (`scripts/net/relay_server.gd`, headless Godot, `--relay`, `make relay`); `RelayPeer` is a `MultiplayerPeerExtension` over a relay room, so RPCs and synchronizers run unchanged; `HostPeer` lets a host listen locally *and* hold the room at once; `LanBeacon` lists games on the same Wi-Fi (not in a browser). Codes: `RelayProtocol.normalize_code`, no look-alike characters. The relay URL is `ashes/network/relay_url` in project.godot (`--relay-url=` overrides; "" = no codes, Wi-Fi and addresses only). Every build talks to every other — browser, desktop, Android, iOS — so never add a transport one of them lacks. `relay_test.gd`, `tools/net_test.sh coop-relay|pvp-relay`.
- **The link is shown, and a dead one is said out loud**: `Net` pings once a second (`link_ms`, `link_silence`), `NetOverlay` (autoload) draws the bars, "connection unstable" after `LINK_STALL`, "<name> left the game" (`Net.partner_left`) and "Connection lost" with the reason (`Net.connection_lost`, `_lost`); `LINK_TIMEOUT` of silence ends it. A guest on another `application/config/version` is refused (`NET_ERR_VERSION`). A co-op night takes late peers (a friend, or one coming back through **Rejoin**, `Net.rejoin_snapshot` = `Saves.capture` of its body): while a match runs on the host, synchronizers are shown only to peers let in (`Net._shown_to`, on every `attach_sync`), and `Run._admit_late` sends the night (`_net_join_state`), then `Net.reveal_to`, then the body. A new replicated node must go through `Net.attach_sync`, or a late peer gets its spawn at the menu and loses it. `tools/net_test.sh coop-rejoin`.

## Run

- **Straight into one room**, for working on it rather than playing to it:
  `/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . scenes/run/run.tscn -- room=hell_gate`
  The name is the scene's own (`rooms` in `data/game.json`, in play order): `village_night`, `graveyard_cross`, `graveyard_arches`, `graveyard_tree`, `swamp_moon`, `swamp_red`, `swamp_crypt`, `catacombs_1`, `catacombs_2`, `catacombs_3`, `crypt_skulls`, `preacher_nave`, `church`, `crypt_lava`, `hell_gate`. An index works too (`room=0` … `room=14`, clamped). No prologue, no intro, hero on his feet — the shape `_resume_from` has. It prints which room it opened, because a mistyped name falls back to the first room and would otherwise look like the argument being ignored. Debug builds only; a release build ignores it, except a studio preview page (`--studio-preview`, `preview-<n>.html?room=…`, which keeps no saves). He arrives as he starts a night — level one, no gifts — so a late room is harder this way than in play. `room_jump_test.gd` covers the parsing.

- **After a pull that touched `assets/`, re-import before believing what you see.** `.import` sidecars and `.godot/imported/` are git-ignored, so a changed PNG keeps its previously imported texture until Godot notices. When a strip's dimensions changed in the same commit as its `cell`, the game draws the *old* texture through the *new* cell and every frame straddles two poses — on screen each enemy looks like it split in two. `validate_data.gd` catches it (it loads the texture, not the file), so a green validator means the import is current. `make import`, or `make room`, which depends on it.

## Design guardrails (see docs/GDD.md)
- Angels ≠ good, demons ≠ evil, God never appears on screen. No "true good ending".
- Core loop stays dumb (move / attack / dash / block / pick a gift). Depth goes into data and story.
- Every dialogue must be skippable. Three looks, one `DialogueBox`: the bottom panel (story choices), captions (`"blocking": false`, over play) and speech bubbles over the speaker's head for talking to NPCs on E (`play_bubble`; Elian's lines and the answers float over him, 1–3 / E / Esc). Only the panel may take `ui_accept` — it used to swallow the Space a cutscene skips on. Where the windows stand, their style and the speaker's portrait are data (docs/DIALOGUE_LOOKS.md): `data/dialogue_looks.json` styles + `speakers`, `"look"` on a dialogue and its nodes, laid on by `DialogueBox._dress` through `DialogueLook`; a dialogue with none keeps the scene's layout. The studio's «Диалоги» drags and styles them and draws them as the box does — change one, change the other. `dialogue_look_test.gd`.
- Chapter I, scene by scene, flag by flag: `docs/CHAPTER1.md`. Keep it in step with `ROOMS`, `data/chapters/` and the scenes.
- The curtain is an autoload (`Curtain`, `scripts/autoload/curtain.gd`) so it outlives the scene under it: it burns a room away, hides the swap, and draws the chapter card when the place changes. Every seam goes through it — `Curtain.change_scene(path, keep_closed, while_black)` for menu/run/duel, `cover()`/`reveal()` around a room swap or the verdict. Places are data: `data/chapters/*.json` lists the rooms of each. Online the host sends `_net_cover`, waits for its own black and only then calls the swap (`Run._go_to_room`) — a peer that is still burning would free the enemies the host just spawned. `Settings.transitions` (full / short / off) scales every beat; a tool script (`godot -s`) gets `Curtain.instant = true` for free, which keeps `_load_room` synchronous.
- Cutscenes are data too: `data/cutscenes/*.json` played by `scripts/ui/cutscene.gd` (`$UI/Cutscene` in the run), attached to rooms by `intro_cutscene` / `outro_cutscene` in the generator tables. A scene `hold`s and must `release`; two presses of any button skip (the first lights the hint, `skip_armed`) and the leftover steps apply instantly. While it holds, the HUD steps out (`cinema()`). A dialogue step's `cast` cuts the camera to each speaker (`DialogueBox.line_shown`); `title` names a boss by its `epithet`; `flash` / `fx` land a beat; `presence` puts eyes in the dark above the fade; panels drift. A scene seen before (`Profile.scenes_seen`, never recorded from a tool script or the yard) skips on one press. Under a spoken line the Music bus steps back `Audio.SPEECH_DUCK_DB`. A room whose scene speaks has no `intro` caption under it. `cinema_test.gd`. `Game.cutscene` is how enemies know to freeze. New step type → `_run` in cutscene.gd **and** `CUTSCENE_STEPS` in the validator. Any step can carry `"if"` / `"unless"` (flags) or `"path"` — that is how one scene plays each choice differently. A choice inside a scene goes through `Run._cutscene_story` (online, the host asks once for both players). A person who exists only for a scene is an NPC with `"scripted": true` (hidden until `appear`, never talked to on E).
